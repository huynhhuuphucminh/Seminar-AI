# Member 1 Q&A practice

> Written with AI assistance (prompt 4.2, see the AI-use log). Answer each question aloud before reading the model answer; in the second round, add the questions of Member 2 and Member 3. Numbers are from the first version (v_max = 2.0); use Report.pdf for the final values.

1. **Where did the idea of PSO come from?** From simulations of bird flocks and fish schools. Kennedy and Eberhart cite Wilson's argument that each fish benefits from discoveries made by the whole school. They treated this sharing of information as the core hypothesis, then removed the parts that only made the simulation look realistic (Kennedy & Eberhart, 1995, pp. 1942–1945).
2. **Why are both pbest and gbest needed?** The 1995 paper reports that both memories were necessary. pbest keeps each particle's own experience and keeps the swarm diverse; gbest spreads good information. If the pull towards gbest is much stronger, the flock rushes into local minima; if pbest dominates, particles wander. Roughly equal pulls worked best (p. 1945).
3. **Why is the coefficient 2 in the original formula?** rand() is uniform on [0, 1], so 2·rand() has mean 1. The authors wanted particles to overshoot their target about half the time (p. 1946).
4. **What is the difference between the 1995 formula and the one you use?** We add the inertia weight ω in front of the old velocity and use c₁ = c₂ = 1.5 instead of 2, plus a velocity limit. This is the inertia-weight form in Poli et al. (2007, Eq. 2). With coefficient 2 and no damping, velocities can grow without control.
5. **Why did you choose ω = 0.7 and c = 1.5?** Poli et al. (2007, Sect. 2.4) show that Clerc's standard constriction setting is equivalent to ω = 0.7298 and c₁ = c₂ ≈ 1.49618. Our default is a rounded version of these values. We also tested ω = 0.4 and 0.9.
6. **What does the inertia weight do physically?** In the review, 1 − ω acts like friction. High ω means low friction, so particles travel far and explore; low ω damps motion, so particles settle and exploit. Many studies decrease ω from about 0.9 to 0.4 during a run (Sect. 2.3).
7. **What is a topology, and which one did you use?** It is the network that decides whose best position a particle can see. We used gbest, where everyone sees the best of the whole swarm. It converges fast, but the lbest ring is less vulnerable to local optima (Sect. 3.1).
8. **How is PSO different from Hill Climbing?** Hill Climbing keeps one point and accepts only better neighbours, so it cannot leave a basin without restarts. PSO keeps a population with memories, accepts every move and shares information. It costs N evaluations per iteration instead of one.
9. **Is the EEG result in the 1995 paper strong evidence that PSO generalises better than backpropagation?** No. The authors themselves call it an "informal indication": one dataset, 92% versus 89%. It is a hint, not a proof.
10. **What weakness do both papers mention?** Premature convergence: a swarm that follows its best member too strongly can settle on a local optimum. The 1995 paper sees it with a high g_increment; the review lists diversity-control variants designed against it (Sect. 4.5).
